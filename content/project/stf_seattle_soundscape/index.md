Passive Acoustic Monitoring (PAM) is a noninvasive method of surveying soniferous animlas using autonomous microphones. In recent years, development of accessible, low-cost devices such as [AudioMoths](https://www.openacousticdevices.info/audiomoth) recorders, as well as the development of machine learning software to detect vocalizations, has greatly impacted the extent to which PAM can be employed; the quantity of data which can be collected has exponentially increased.

The Greater Seattle Soundscape Monitoring Project seeks to engage students and community members from the University of Washington and Greater Seattle area in a citizen-science program. Participants are responsible using loaned AudioMoth devices to collect and analyze data, which adds to a growing dataset and contributes to creating a "biodiversity fingerprint" of various locations across the Seattle. The ultimate goal is for the project to serve as an extension to our similar [long-term effort](/project/ubna-pam/) in the Union Bay Natural Area.

By engaging with bioacoustics technology and research, students from diverse academic backgrounds develop strong interdisciplinary skills across a variety of fields (including acoustics, ecology, data science, and signal processing). This accessible entry point also serves to improve technical skills and create open pathways for students to generate conclusions from large datsets in a setting outside the classroom. The program aims to not restrict access and requires no prior knowledge of bioacoustics, data science, or research.

See the [Greater Seattle Soundscape Monitoring Project](seattle-soundscape.uw.edu) website for more information about joining the program and generated results.
