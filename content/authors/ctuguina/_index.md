I'm a second year Electrical Engineering PhD student working on acoustic projects at the University of Washington's Applied Physics Lab advised by Dr. Wu-Jung Lee. My research focuses on the analysis of echosounder data (high-frequency active sonar systems), specifically in the field of fisheries acoustics. I'm currently working on adapting computer vision models for acoustic identification of Pacific Hake, a semi-demersal fish species and one of the largest fish stocks along the U.S. West Coast. The current focus of this work is developing a mechanistic understanding of how the models make their predictions, specifically identifying which parts of the “context,” such as the presence of seafloor or other biological scatterers, the models use to predict hake.

To support this effort, I'm working on two open-source packages. I'm developing and maintaining [Echoregions](https://github.com/OSOceanAcoustics/echoregions): An open-source software package that interfaces annotations and water column sonar data for training machine learning models. I'm also helping in the effort to develop [Echopype](https://github.com/OSOceanAcoustics/echopype): An open-source software package that is designed to parse, calibrate, regrid, and perform other scientific computations on large amounts of raw acoustic data from various commercial and scientific echosounder brands.
